About me

I am a John N. Bahcall Fellow in the School of Natural Sciences at the Institute for Advanced Study. Previously I was a Leverhulme–Peierls Fellow at the Rudolf Peierls Centre for Theoretical Physics, University of Oxford, where I completed my DPhil in 2022 with Steven Balbus.

I work on the physics of gas falling onto black holes. The focus is regimes that standard thin-disc models usually skip: time-dependent relativistic discs, and the plunging region inside the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO). I combine analytic theory with observations and GRMHD simulations to infer black hole masses and spins, and to interpret tidal disruption events (TDEs) and X-ray binaries.

TDEs are a particular observational laboratory: a star is shredded near a supermassive black hole, a disc forms, and the system evolves on months to years rather than millions of years. That makes it possible to watch an accretion flow turn on, spread, change state, and (sometimes) launch outflows. The same disc theory is used on stellar-mass black holes in X-ray binaries, including emission from within the ISCO.

Codes from this work are public, including FitTeD (light curves and spectra of evolving relativistic discs), TDEFLARE (rapid TDE black hole masses from optical/UV flares), and XSPEC models for plunging-region and TDE disc spectra (Software).
